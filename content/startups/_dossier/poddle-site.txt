Pickleball game for PC, Mac and Chromebook: nothing to install 
 Poddle 
 Play 
 Pickleball on a PC, Mac or Chromebook, nothing to install
 Poddle is a pickleball game that runs in a browser tab. Your phone is the paddle, and the paddle is a web page too. There is no app store and nothing to install on the computer or the phone.
 One exception. To use an AirPod as the paddle instead of a phone, a Mac needs the free Poddle Helper app.
 Play Poddle 
 What you need, and what you do not
 You need
 A computer or an iPad with a current browser
 A phone with motion sensors, including a gyroscope (or, on a Mac, one AirPod with Poddle Helper)
 An internet connection on each device you use
 You do not need
 An app, a plugin or a download for the phone paddle
 An account or an email address
 A webcam, a controller or the same Wi-Fi on both devices
 Windows, Mac, Chromebook, Linux or iPad
 Any computer that runs a current browser can be the screen: Windows, macOS, Chromebook (ChromeOS) or Linux. An iPad works too. The browser needs JavaScript and WebGL turned on, because WebGL draws the 3D court.
 The AirPod option needs a Mac on macOS 14 or later, Google Chrome, AirPods Pro, AirPods 3, AirPods 4 or AirPods Max, and Poddle Helper; its AirPod setup steps are in How to play.
 The phone
 An iPhone or an Android phone with motion sensors, including a gyroscope. The paddle reads how fast the phone turns, and a phone without a gyroscope cannot report that. Scanning the QR code on the computer opens the paddle page in the phone's own browser. On an iPhone, the page asks for permission to use motion. Choose Allow.
 The paddle page asks the browser to keep the screen awake while you play. If the browser has no way to do that, the page shows a tip instead: set Auto-Lock to Never while you play.
 What runs in the browser
 The game, its fonts and its sounds are served from poddleball.com and run inside the tab. Each visit loads the current version, so there is no update to install and nothing left behind when you close the tab, apart from your settings and a random device identifier kept in the browser's own storage.
 The phone and the computer never connect to each other directly, which is why they can be on different networks. Each one opens its own connection to the Poddle server.
 A webcam is optional
 The webcam is on the computer, not the phone. It lets you move your player by stepping. Without one, choose Play without camera, or set Move to Auto, and the game runs you to the ball while you swing. How Move works is in How to play.
 The body tracking is Google's open-source MediaPipe software, served from poddleball.com. It loads into the tab only after you allow the camera, and it reads the video inside the browser on your computer. The video is never recorded, saved or sent. Only your approximate position goes to the game.
 If the browser blocks a sensor
 The phone says Motion is switched off
 On an iPhone, motion access was refused with Don't Allow. Close the tab, scan the code again, and choose Allow. If the phone never asked, it may lack a gyroscope. Try another phone.
 The phone says No motion sensors here
 On a laptop, this is expected: open the paddle page on your phone. On an Android phone, Chrome may be blocking motion for this site. In Chrome's site settings for poddleball.com, set Motion sensors to Allow, then tap Try again.
 The computer says the camera is blocked
 Allow the camera in your browser's site settings. The game shows the steps for
